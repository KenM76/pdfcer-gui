# `ui-verify/checks/compact_pictures`

`a_compacted_copy_can_make_pictures_smaller` — **the compact window's picture
option reaches the engine, says what it lost, and is what Save writes.**

## What it drives

Opens `fixtures/noisy-photo.pdf` (one 400×400 grey noise picture drawn 96 pt
square: 300 ppi) with `PDFCER_DIAG_INVOKE=mode.edit,file.save_compacted` and
`PDFCER_DIAG_SAVE_PATH` pointing into the run's output folder. It waits for the
first `compact-recompressed` line (the window recompresses on open), clicks the
*Make pictures smaller* fold (`compact.pictures`), clicks its switch
(`compact.pictures.on`), waits for the next `compact-recompressed` line, then
clicks Save (`compact.commit`).

## What it asserts, in order

| # | assertion | what its failure means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | the baseline line has `resized=0` | pictures were changed without being asked for |
| 2 | the fold and its switch are declared visible | the option is missing or clipped |
| 3 | a new line follows the switch with `resized≥1` | the choice never reached the engine's plan |
| 4 | its `bytes` are fewer than the baseline's | scaling saved nothing |
| 5 | `compact.pictures.lossy` is drawn exactly when the line says `lossy=true` | the loss of detail goes unsaid, or is claimed falsely |
| 6 | the written file's length equals that line's `bytes` | Save wrote a different copy from the one measured |

## Why the fixture is noise

A flat scan scaled down compresses *worse*, and the engine rightly keeps the
original (`re-encoding them was not smaller`); `synthetic-image-only.pdf` does
exactly that. Noise does not compress, so scaling it is what shrinks the copy.
Generated by `fixtures/noisy-photo.PROVENANCE.py`.

## Falsification

Arms 3, 5 and 2 (for Save) have each been seen red against real defects: the
flat-scan fixture (arm 3), the warning scrolled below the window's edge
(arm 5), and the opened fold pushing Save out of the window (the Save arm).
The clipping arms measure egui's clip rectangle, not a screenshot: the window
is its own viewport, which the harness does not capture.

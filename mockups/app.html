Your answer was “both”. The Format tab carries the edits you reach for mid-gesture; the properties panel carries the full set and survives a tab switch. Right-click gives the same commands a third way. Today none of these three exist: selecting an object produces a tree row and nothing else.

2 · View tab — render options, as you asked

You found whole-page raster faster and more pleasant to pan and zoom than the tiled competitor. That is a real trade, not a weakness: one texture scaled during the settle gives smooth motion with no tile seams and no progressive fill-in. So the answer is not to replace it — it is to expose the choice, which is what R169 says to do with anything genuinely undetermined.

  • Whole page stays the defaultIt is what you measured as better. Tiled is the opt-in for sheets big enough that time-to-first-pixel matters more than smoothness.

3 · Editing — no master switch, the way other programs work

Your answer: make it work the way other programs do. So the global Editing on toggle goes away entirely. Selection and Delete always work. Picking a tool arms that tool, and the tool disarms when you press Escape or pick another. There is no state in which clicking does nothing without saying so.

5 · Measure — the tab that is one group today

The dimension-group model (named groups carrying a shared scale and drafting standard) is better than anything the competitor offers and stays exactly as it is. What is missing is Area and Angular, which are the two a takeoff needs, and Two-line — which is already done in core and CLI and simply has no caller on the canvas.

Six of ten markup kinds are deferred in the code today, and Cloud is not even on that list. It is the single most-used markup on a drawing revision. The Style group sets the next markup’s appearance; changing a placed one is the Format tab’s job.
